What to look for

  • Clean cut vs. max cut: the clean-cut number is the thickness you'll get a usable edge on. The max (sever) number is for chopping stock apart, not finished parts.
  • Pilot arc: the arc starts without touching the metal, so you can cut rusty, painted or expanded metal and your tips last longer.
  • 120V vs. 240V: dual-voltage machines lose power on a household outlet. Plan thick cuts around 240V.
  • Air supply: plasma runs on compressed air, and it has to be clean and dry. Check the machine's psi range against your compressor and add a filter or dryer.
  • Consumables: tips and electrodes wear out. Pick a torch whose parts are easy to buy.
  • CNC later? A hand-held cutter without a CNC port can't run a table. Decide now if a table is in your future.
